Abstract

Robot world models simulate how environments evolve in response to robot actions. Latent action models enable learning dynamics from videos by encoding visual transitions as proxy actions. However, reconstruction-driven latent actions mix robot motion with object responses and other scene changes, complicating their correspondence with robot controls. We propose SkeLAM, a skeleton-guided latent action model for controllable robot world modeling. SkeLAM decomposes latent actions into a kinematic component grounded by robot skeleton supervision and a residual component retaining complementary visual dynamics. Cross-modal swap reconstruction encourages RGB-derived and skeleton-derived kinematic representations to be interchangeable during video reconstruction. The world model incorporates both components through separate conditioning pathways, while branch dropout reduces its dependence on future-derived residual information. A lightweight adapter maps robot actions to the kinematic space, and subsequent world-model fine-tuning adapts generation to these predicted conditions. At inference, the model generates future observations from the current observation and robot actions. On DROID, SkeLAM reduces end-effector ADE by 19.5% and LPIPS by 11.1% compared with DreamDojo. Motion probing supports kinematic specialization, while qualitative results demonstrate zero-shot human-to-robot motion transfer in generated rollouts. Policy evaluation shows agreement between predicted success rates and those measured in real robot executions.

Method

Skeleton-grounded latent actions. SkeLAM encodes RGB transitions into a 32D kinematic latent and a 32D residual latent. Paired robot skeletons supervise the kinematic component; cross-modal swap reconstruction encourages RGB and skeleton motion representations to be interchangeable.

World-model conditioning. The two components use separate projection pathways in Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B. Branch dropout reduces reliance on residual information derived from future observations.

Action adaptation. A lightweight adapter maps robot actions to the kinematic space. At inference, the model generates future observations from the current RGB image and robot actions, with the residual set to zero.

Experimental Results

Trained on only ~10 hours of robot data with a pretrained world-model backbone, SkeLAM ranks first on seven of eight reported DROID metrics among action-conditioned models. Ctrl-World and OSCAR achieve higher direction F1.

Zero-Shot Human-to-Robot Motion Transfer

After training on ~10 hours of robot data, SkeLAM transfers human hand motions to generated robot rollouts zero-shot, with no additional fine-tuning. With all parameters frozen, the RGB encoder extracts latent actions from human demonstrations in UniSkill; these latents condition a robot rollout from its own initial observation.

Open-Loop Policy Evaluation

We evaluate seven policies across 15 RoboArena sessions (105 episodes). Both world models receive the same initial observations and recorded actions. Human annotators label success in the generated rollouts for comparison with real executions.

SkeLAM achieves 63.02% balanced accuracy and 33.33% success recall. Policy-level Spearman correlation is 0.5347, and success-rate MAE decreases from 7.62 to 4.76 percentage points compared with Cosmos-Predict2.5.

The real episodes contain 9 successes and 96 failures. These results concern recorded-action replay; closed-loop policy evaluation remains future work.
